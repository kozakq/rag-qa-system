Skyrim's main story draws on lore established across the earlier Elder Scrolls games. Alduin,
called the World-Eater, is a dragon prophesied in ancient texts to return and bring about the
end of the world by consuming it. In Nordic mythology within the game, Alduin was the first-born
of the deity Akatosh and was banished into the timeless void long ago by a group of ancient
Nord heroes known as the Tongues, using an ability called the Thu'um, or dragon shout, along
with a weapon called the Elder Scroll. Alduin's return at the start of Skyrim sets the main
questline in motion: the player, as the Dragonborn -- a mortal born with the soul and power of a
dragon -- must learn to use the Thu'um and eventually confront Alduin directly.

The province of Skyrim is home to the Nords, a hardy human culture that reveres strength,
tradition, and the old gods, chief among them the Nine Divines (with Talos, a deified former
emperor, being especially controversial since the Aldmeri Dominion has banned his worship
across Tamriel following the Great War). Skyrim's history includes the Dragon War, in which
ancient dragons ruled over mortals until Nord heroes overthrew them, and the founding of the
kingdom by the legendary hero Ysgramor after the Night of Tears, when the elven Snow Elves
massacred the first Nord settlement of Saarthal.

At the time the game takes place, Skyrim is embroiled in a civil war between the Empire,
represented by the Legion, and the Stormcloaks, a rebel faction led by Ulfric Stormcloak who
seeks independence for Skyrim and the right to freely worship Talos. This conflict runs in
parallel to the main dragon-slaying questline, and the player can choose to side with either
faction, remain neutral, or complete the main story without resolving the war at all. The
tension between old Nord tradition and the political reality of an Empire weakened by war with
the elves is a recurring theme throughout Skyrim's writing.

Other significant lore threads include the Daedric Princes -- powerful otherworldly entities
such as Molag Bal, Hircine, and Mephala -- who offer the player quests in exchange for service,
and the Dwemer (Deep Elves), an ancient and technologically advanced elven civilization that
vanished under mysterious circumstances long before the game's events, leaving behind vast
underground ruins full of steam-powered constructs.
