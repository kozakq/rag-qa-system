Skyrim departs from the class-based character creation of earlier Elder Scrolls games in favor
of a fully freeform system. There are no character classes; instead, the player has eighteen
skills split across three categories -- Warrior (One-Handed, Two-Handed, Heavy Armor, Block,
Smithing, etc.), Mage (Destruction, Restoration, Illusion, Conjuration, Alteration, Enchanting),
and Thief (Sneak, Lockpicking, Pickpocket, Speech, Alchemy, Light Armor) -- and each skill
levels up simply through use. Leveling a skill contributes to the character's overall level, and
each level-up grants a perk point that can be spent in a large skill-specific perk tree to
unlock specialized abilities, such as increased sneak-attack damage or the ability to dual-cast
spells for amplified effects.

Combat supports a flexible mix of melee weapons, ranged weapons (primarily bows), and magic,
and the game allows dual-wielding of one-handed weapons or spells in each hand, including
combining a weapon in one hand with a spell in the other. Shouts, or Thu'um, are Skyrim's
signature magic-like ability unique to the Dragonborn: each shout is composed of up to three
runic "words of power," written in the ancient dragon language, which must be found inscribed
on Word Walls scattered throughout the world and then unlocked by absorbing a dragon's soul
after defeating it. Notable shouts include Unrelenting Force (a knockback attack), Fire
Breath, and Become Ethereal.

Outside combat, Skyrim emphasizes exploration and crafting. Smithing lets players forge and
improve weapons and armor at forges and grindstones; Alchemy allows combining ingredients
gathered in the world to brew potions and poisons; and Enchanting lets players imbue
weapons, armor, and jewelry with magical effects using soul gems, which store souls captured
from slain creatures (optionally using the Soul Trap spell). The game also features a radiant
quest system that procedurally generates repeatable side quests -- such as clearing out a
bandit camp or retrieving a specific item -- to give players an effectively endless supply of
minor objectives alongside the hand-authored main and faction questlines.

Dragons in Skyrim are dynamic world encounters rather than purely scripted set pieces: after a
certain point in the main quest, dragons can randomly appear and attack while the player is
exploring the overworld, adding an element of unpredictability to travel between locations.
