The Elder Scrolls V: Skyrim is an open-world action role-playing game developed by Bethesda
Game Studios and published by Bethesda Softworks. It was released on November 11, 2011, for
Windows, PlayStation 3, and Xbox 360, with later remastered releases -- Skyrim Special Edition
(2016) and Skyrim Anniversary Edition (2021) -- bringing it to PlayStation 4, Xbox One,
Nintendo Switch, and current-generation consoles. It is the fifth main installment in The
Elder Scrolls series, following Oblivion (2006), and was directed by Todd Howard.

The game is set two hundred years after the events of Oblivion, in the northern province of
Skyrim, home to the Nords. The player character, referred to as the Dragonborn, arrives in
Skyrim as a prisoner about to be executed, only to escape when the ancient dragon Alduin
attacks the town of Helgen. From there the game opens into a fully explorable world of roughly
sixteen square miles, containing cities, ruins, dungeons, and wilderness that the player can
approach in almost any order.

Skyrim received widespread critical acclaim for its open-ended design, freeform character
progression, and the sheer density of hand-crafted content packed into its world. It won
numerous Game of the Year awards in 2011 and has sold over 30 million copies across all its
releases, making it one of the best-selling video games of all time. Its score, composed by
Jeremy Soule, is closely associated with the "Dragonborn" choral theme heard in the main menu
and during dragon encounters.

Three official expansions were released for the original game: Dawnguard (vampires and the
Dawnguard order), Hearthfire (home-building and adoption), and Dragonborn (set on the island of
Solstheim, revisiting the Red Mountain and the Skaal). The Anniversary Edition later folded in
these expansions along with dozens of smaller Creation Club add-ons into a single package.
